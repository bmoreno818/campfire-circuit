Camping Power: Power Stations vs. Power Banks

Before you buy, figure out how much energy you actually use. Battery capacity is measured in watt-hours (Wh). Add up what you'll charge each day: a phone, a headlamp, maybe a camera, a fan, or a CPAP. Then multiply by the number of nights and leave a buffer of about 20%, since inverters and cables lose some power.

Step 1: Size your needs

  • Phones and small gadgets only: a large USB-C power bank is lighter and cheaper.
  • Laptops, lights, a 12V fridge or cooler, and multiple people: a portable power station.
  • Trips of several days: add solar so you're recharging, not just draining.

Step 2: Battery chemistry matters

LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) batteries generally handle many more charge cycles than standard lithium-ion and are considered more thermally stable. That makes them a good fit for gear that gets used every season.

Tips to get more out of any battery

  • Charge devices over USB-C when you can, because DC to DC wastes less energy than going through an inverter.
  • Angle solar panels toward the sun and readjust them every couple of hours. Even light shade cuts output sharply.
  • In cold weather, keep batteries in your tent or sleeping bag. Capacity drops when they get cold.
