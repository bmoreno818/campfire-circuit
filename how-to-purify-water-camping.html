How to Purify Water While Camping

Even clear mountain streams can carry bacteria and protozoa such as Giardia and Cryptosporidium. Here are the main methods and when to use each one.

1. Boiling

A rolling boil kills pathogens. The CDC recommends 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 ft (about 2,000 m). Boiling is reliable but uses fuel and time, and it does nothing for sediment or taste.

2. Hollow-fiber filters

Filters physically strain out bacteria and protozoa. They're fast and use no fuel. Most do not remove viruses, which matters more for international travel or water near heavy human use.

3. Chemical treatment

Chlorine dioxide tablets or drops are light and handle viruses. They take a while to work, so follow the manufacturer's wait times, which are longer for cold water and Cryptosporidium. They make a good backup to a filter.

Field tips

  • Collect from flowing water and pre-filter murky water through a bandana.
  • Keep "dirty" and "clean" containers and threads separate.
  • Don't let hollow-fiber filters freeze. Ice can crack the fibers without any visible damage. Sleep with the filter in winter.
