Camp Lighting That Actually Works

Good camp lighting is about having the right light for each job, not the brightest one.

Lumens, roughly

  • Under 100 lumens: tent reading and camp chores up close.
  • 100–300: general camp use and walking on trails.
  • 300+: route-finding at night and searching for things far away.

Headlamp + lantern = the system

A headlamp lights wherever you look, so you can cook and keep your hands free. A lantern fills the tent or picnic table with soft, even light and keeps you from blinding everyone with your headlamp.

Headlamp tips

  • Choose one with a red mode. It protects night vision and is polite to your neighbors.
  • Look for a lockout feature so it doesn't turn on in your pack.
  • Check the water resistance rating if you camp in the rain.

Etiquette

Point lights down, switch to red around other people's tents, and turn off lanterns at quiet hours.
